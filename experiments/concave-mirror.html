Concave Mirror Lab

Aim

To find the value of v for different values of u in case of a concave mirror, and to determine its focal length, using an optical bench.

Underlying principle

A concave mirror forms a real, inverted image of an object placed beyond its focus. The pole-to-object distance u and pole-to-image distance v are related to the focal length f by the mirror formula:

1/f  =  1/v  +  1/u      f = uv / (u + v)

By the sign convention used in this practical, both u and v are measured as negative distances from the pole, so the computed f also turns out negative.

uDistance of the object needle from the pole of the mirror.
vDistance of the image needle from the pole of the mirror, located by removing parallax.
fFocal length, found for each trial and then averaged / found graphically.

How this virtual lab works

Open the 3D Experiment tab to manipulate a virtual optical bench modelled on the real apparatus, always shown as your main workspace. Drag the object needle to set u, then drag the image needle until the tip-to-tip parallax with the real image is removed — exactly as you would with your eye at the real bench. Record the reading, repeat for several values of u, and every other tab — Observations, Calculations, Graph, Result, Precautions, Viva — will open in the side panel next to the bench, building itself automatically from what you actually measured.

Apparatus Required

An optical bench with three uprights (one fixed near the zero end, two outer uprights with lateral movement), a concave mirror in its holder, two optical needles — one thin (object needle), one thick (image needle) — a knitting needle for the rough focal-length check, and a half-metre scale.

Precautions

  • Keep the principal axis of the mirror horizontal and parallel to the length of the optical bench.
  • Keep all uprights vertical.
  • Remove tip-to-tip parallax carefully between the image needle and the image of the object needle.
  • View the image from at least 30 cm away for a reliable parallax check.
  • Keep the tips of both needles at the same height as the pole of the mirror.
  • Apply index correction to both u and v before using them in calculations.

Sources of Error

  • The uprights may not be perfectly vertical.
  • Parallax removal is never perfect and introduces small errors.

Viva Voce / Self-Check

Why must the object be placed beyond the focus?

Only then does the concave mirror produce a real, inverted image that can be caught on a screen or located with an image needle. If the object is between the pole and focus, the image becomes virtual and cannot be located this way.

What is "parallax" and why does removing it matter here?

Parallax is the apparent relative shift between two objects at different distances when the observer's eye moves sideways. Removing it between the image needle and the real image confirms both lie at the same distance from the eye — meaning the needle now genuinely marks the image's location.

Why are u and v taken as negative?

By the Cartesian sign convention used for mirrors, distances measured against the direction of incident light (i.e., on the same side as the object) are taken as negative, with the pole as origin.

Why is the u–v graph a rectangular hyperbola?

Because u and v satisfy 1/u + 1/v = 1/f, a constant — this relation traces a rectangular hyperbola when u is plotted against v.

What does the 1/u vs 1/v graph give directly?

A straight line with equal, opposite-sign intercepts on both axes, each equal in magnitude to 1/f — so the focal length can be read directly from either intercept.

Why must the needle tips be at the same height as the pole?

So that the object and its image both lie on the principal axis, making u and v true axial distances rather than slant distances.
